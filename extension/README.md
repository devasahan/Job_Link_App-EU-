# JobBridge EU Chrome Extension

Standalone extension — **no `web.app` / Flask server required**.

Fetches **remote developer jobs open to people living in the EU** from job boards' public APIs, then downloads an Excel (`.xlsx`) file: jobs based in the EU countries you tick, and jobs at employers in the UK, Canada and the US that say they hire in Europe. Built from JobBridge Fetch 1.14.0 (remote US jobs). Every ticked source runs at the same time, each with its own time budget; the same job found on several sources is one row. More sources plug into the same pipeline (see **Adding a board**).

## Platforms

| Option | How it fetches |
|--------|----------------|
| Indeed | GraphQL `apis.indeed.com`, one search per ticked country that has an Indeed site, plus the UK, Canada and US sites for **Employers abroad**: the `indeed-co` header picks the country (`DE`, `FR`, `GB`, `US`…); labels come back in English (`indeed-locale: en-US`, as JobSpy sent until Sep 2026 and omkarcloud/indeed-scraper sends for every country). Remote jobs only: Indeed's remote attribute `DSQF7` and not its hybrid one `PAXZC`. The **Posted** window is applied by Indeed (`dateOnIndeed`) and checked again here. Indeed's newer filter list is tried first; if Indeed rejects it, the run says so and steps down — without the hybrid exclusion, then the remote filter alone (the US version's shape) — and checks the rest itself. With no **Title**, each country is searched for "developer" and its own word for it (`Entwickler`, `développeur`, `programista`, `utvecklare`…; "software engineer" in English-speaking countries). A typed title is searched as typed and, outside English-speaking countries, with the country's word for developer in place of the English one (`Backend Developer` → `Backend Entwickler`, `Software Engineer` → `Software Entwickler`; `Data Engineer` stays English). Employers abroad are searched with each of those plus `Europe`, `EMEA` and `worldwide`, since most of their remote jobs are local. Up to 5 pages of 100 per search, newest first, one request at a time (0.5 s between pages, 1 s between countries, 20 s timeout). The job type is read from each job's attribute keys. A job Indeed places in an EU country you didn't tick is left out. Links point at the country's site (`de.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=…`, `uk.indeed.com` for the UK, `www.indeed.com` for the US). An Indeed 401 (app key refused), 403 or 429 stops Indeed for that run; other errors skip only that country. Country codes and subdomains follow [JobSpy](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)'s `jobspy/indeed/constant.py` |
| Himalayas | Its public JSON API (`himalayas.app/jobs/api/search?sort=recent&page=N`, no key), 20 remote jobs a page, newest first, one request every 0.8 s. With **You live in** set it asks for jobs open to that country (`country=SE`, which includes worldwide jobs); a single typed **Title** is sent as its search (`q`) and **Type** as `employment_type`; every result is checked here anyway. A job is open to the countries in its `locationRestrictions` (none listed: worldwide), narrowed by its `timezoneRestrictions` (a job for UTC-8 to UTC-5 is left out). Expired jobs (`expiryDate`) are left out. Paging stops when a whole page is older than **Posted**, at the last page, or at Count. A header rule sends a plain User-Agent for the extension's own API requests (Himalayas refuses browser-like ones). Jobs link to himalayas.app, as its terms ask, with the employer's application link as Direct URL |

## Countries

The popup lists the 27 EU member states (all ticked by default) and Norway and Switzerland as extras (unticked): remote jobs based in the ticked countries are kept. **All EU** / **None** pick quickly.

Indeed has a site for 18 EU countries — Austria, Belgium, Czechia, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Spain, Sweden — and for Norway and Switzerland. Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Slovakia and Slovenia have none: they are greyed out, the popup summary counts them ("All EU (27) · 9 not searched") and names them under the list, and the run log says they were not searched. Jobs abroad that are open to Europe or to them still count.

## Employers abroad

**United Kingdom**, **Canada** and **United States** (all ticked by default): their remote jobs count only when the posting says the employer hires where you are. The title, location and description are read one at a time (a phrase never runs from one field into the next) and without the company's own name (`Zap Solutions Europe`); each place a posting names is read with the words around it.

- **Open**: Europe, the EU, EEA or EMEA as a tag or where candidates live ("Remote (Europe)", "EU/UK Based - Remote", "UK + EU remote", "candidates based in EMEA", "must be physically based within EMEA", "remote for candidates located in the United States, Canada, and Europe", "we welcome applications from across the UK and Europe", "must be based in the UK or an EU country", "we can't hire outside the EU"), also in a bullet that runs into the next one ("Based in the EU" then "5+ years of Go"); a region in the title or location field ("(US or EMEA)", "US, Europe, LatAm"); worldwide ("100% Remote, Worldwide", "anywhere in the world", "your location is not a requirement", "we hire wherever you are", "candidates based outside the US"); an employer of record ("non-US employees are hired through an Employer of Record", "employed through Oyster"); or one of your ticked countries, in English or its own language, alone or in a list ("Remote – Germany", "candidates in Polska", "candidates based in the UK, Germany, Sweden or Spain", "UK & ROI").
- **European time zones** ("CET", "European and U.S. time zones", "UTC-3 to UTC+3", "GMT time zone (or +/- 3 hours)") count when nothing closes the job.
- **Closed**: a restriction to the employer's country ("US only", "must reside in the United States", "Remote (US)", "Remote - Anywhere in Canada", "Canada-wide", "This role will be remote and based in the United States", "not open to other countries", "we don't currently hire outside the US"), or a hire it rules out ("not open to candidates in the EU", "we do not hire in Europe"). Citizenship, a green card or a clearance (a Public Trust clearance, SC, DV, TS/SCI, ITAR, export-controlled work, "U.S. Person") closes it whatever else it says; "green card sponsorship", "public trust in elections" and an "export controls screening service" are not clearances. When a posting says both, only an open title or location field keeps it ("Software Quality Engineer (EU - Remote)"); a home tag joined to a European one ("Remote (US/Canada) or Remote (Europe)") is the other half of the hire, not a restriction.
- Home work authorization ("authorized to work in the U.S.", "right to work in the UK") and W2 / 1099 / C2C don't cancel an open signal, since they are written for the home half of a mixed hire, but they do cancel a time zone or a loose mention ("you'll collaborate with engineers in Europe"); US or UK hours count against a job only when it names no European time zone, and an offset that spells them out is home time ("UK hours (UTC+0 in winter and UTC+1 in summer)"). A "must" counts only in the last words before a place, so "requires close collaboration with engineers in the US" restricts nothing.
- **Not signals**: company blurbs ("offices in London and Berlin", "customers in 120 countries", "our team members work across North America and Europe", "a team of 120 people across the US, Canada and Europe", "we have engineers across Europe", "a team spread from PST to CET", "Europe is our biggest market", "an EU-based payments provider", "one of the best places to work in Europe", "make care accessible to everyone in the world", "the leading employer of record platform"), someone or something else that is based there ("our VP, who is based in Germany", "banks and insurers based in the UK", "data centers located in the EU", "revenue from customers outside the US"), travel ("events across the US, Canada and Europe", "trips outside the U.S."), perks ("work from anywhere in Europe for up to 4 weeks a year", "four weeks a year to work from anywhere in the world"), US state notices ("for candidates located in California, the salary range is…") and EMEA as a sales territory ("customers across the EMEA region"). "Work from anywhere" alone usually means anywhere in the employer's country and is left out, as is a posting that says nothing: most remote jobs there are local.

With **You live in** set, "where you are" is that country: a job abroad counts when it is open to Europe, the EU, EEA, EMEA, worldwide or your country, whatever else you ticked. A country list opens the job only for the ticked countries it names (Ireland in "US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand"; Northern Ireland is the UK), a part of Europe only for its countries (`DACH`, `Nordics`, `Benelux`, `CEE`…), and a time-zone window only for the ticked countries inside it ("UTC+1 to UTC-8" leaves out Finland, Greece, Romania and the other UTC+2 countries); "must reside in Ireland or the UK" narrows a wider tag such as "(EMEA)". The sheet's **Open to** column says who a job abroad was kept for (`Europe`, `EMEA`, `Worldwide`, `European time zones`, `Germany, Sweden, Spain`, `European time zones (UTC-8 to UTC+1)`…).

## Load in Chrome

1. Open `chrome://extensions`
2. Enable **Developer mode**
3. **Load unpacked** → select this `extension/` folder
4. Pin **JobBridge EU** → set platforms / countries / employers abroad / count / type / years / title / stack → **OK**
5. Keep the fetch tab open until the Excel download finishes

If you already had the extension loaded, click **Reload** on the card after updates.

## Popup fields

- **Platforms** — sources to search, in groups (**Job boards** for now; company career pages, freelance and recruitment agencies come later), each with a count ("Job boards · 2 of 2") and **All** / **None**: click a group to open it. A source added in an update starts ticked or not as it comes (busy or slow ones start off), even if you saved your choices before. Freelance sources are greyed out while **Type** is Full time and skipped in the run.
- **You live in** — optional, not set by default. Set it to get jobs from other countries only: your own country isn't searched (it is greyed out in **Countries**), jobs another site lists there are left out, and so are remote jobs elsewhere that say you must live in, or work from within, their country or a place that leaves yours out — "100% Homeoffice innerhalb Deutschlands", "deutschlandweit", "Wohnsitz in Deutschland", "dass du in Deutschland wohnst", "Remote (Deutschland)" or "(nur DE)" in the title, "télétravail depuis la France", "100% remoto desde España", "praca zdalna z terenu Polski", "must be based in the Netherlands", "Remote within Ireland", "Remote in DACH", "within commuting distance of our Dublin office", a Swiss or Luxembourg job that turns away cross-border commuters ("Grenzgänger leider ausgeschlossen") — in the languages of the Indeed sites it searches, and English. Each sentence and list item is read on its own. Jobs that don't say where you must live stay, as do jobs open to the EU, Europe, the EEA, EMEA, the world, abroad or a list that names your country ("Remote aus ganz Europa", "Remote in Deutschland oder Schweden", "auch aus dem Ausland möglich"; cross-border commuters welcome opens a Swiss job to its neighbours). Not a requirement: the company, its offices and addresses ("Sitz in Berlin"), its team ("wir arbeiten remote aus ganz Deutschland"), customers, products or markets there, even "only" ("Kunden nur in Deutschland", "nicht nur in Deutschland"), travel, languages ("fließend Deutsch"), a right to work ("Arbeitserlaubnis für Deutschland"; EU citizens have it), office visits, perks ("Workation: bis zu 30 Tage im Jahr aus dem EU-Ausland"), a preference ("ideally based in Ireland", "von Vorteil"), and Indeed's own location field ("Remote in Deutschland" is Indeed's label, not the employer's).
- **Countries** — where a remote job may be based (see **Countries**). The summary shows "All EU (27)" or the names you picked, and how many of them no ticked source can search (remote boards such as Himalayas search every country, so with one ticked none is greyed out); click it to change the list.
- **Employers abroad** — UK, Canada and US employers whose remote jobs say they hire in Europe (see **Employers abroad**).
- **Count per source** — max results **per source** (1–200), across all countries, newest first. One country can fill it. A job found on several sources counts for the one that keeps it (see **Notes**).
- **Posted** — **Today** (last 24h), **3 days**, **7 days** or **30 days**. Remote developer jobs posted in one EU country in a single day are few, so a longer window finds many more.
- **Type** — **Full time**, **Contract / freelance** or **Both**. Both keeps full-time and contract jobs (and jobs that never say a type) and drops part-time ones. Internships, traineeships, apprenticeships, working-student, temp-agency, seasonal and volunteer jobs are always dropped, even when Indeed tags them full time (`Werkstudent Softwareentwicklung`, `Software Engineer Intern`, `Stage Développeur`); "Werkstudent möglich" (an option) doesn't count. With Full time or Contract, a job that clearly says the other type is dropped; jobs that never say a type stay. The type is read from Indeed's attribute keys, then the title, then explicit phrases or Indeed's "Job Types:" footer in the description, in any EU language (`Vollzeit`, `Teilzeit`, `Werkstudent`, `Praktikum`, `temps plein`, `stage`, `alternance`, `prácticas`, `tirocinio`, `deeltijd`, `pełny etat`, `staż`, `freiberuflich`, `umowa B2B`, `partita IVA`…). Permanent and fixed-term jobs (`Festanstellung`, `befristet`, `CDI`, `CDD`, `vast contract`) are employee jobs, so they count as full time unless they say part time. `Voll- oder Teilzeit` and `UoP lub B2B` are either. Swiss workloads count too (`80–100%` is either, `60%` part time; `100% remote` and `+ 15% Bonus` aren't workloads). In descriptions only phrases about the job count (`permanent position` is full time, `permanent residents` isn't). Jobs *about* a type aren't of that type: `Contract Manager`, `Stage Manager`, `Ausbildungsleiter`, `Early-Stage Startup`; neither are company footers (`Partita IVA 0123…`) or passing mentions (`we brief our freelancers`).
- **Years** — optional. Enter `5` to keep jobs whose posting asks for **5.5 years or fewer**: `5+ years of experience`, `3-5 years experience`, `Experience: 5+ years`, `mindestens 5 Jahre Berufserfahrung`, `Berufserfahrung (mind. 5 Jahre)`, `5 ans d'expérience`, `almeno 5 anni di esperienza`, `minimaal 5 jaar werkervaring`, `min. 5 lat doświadczenia`, `minst 5 års erfarenhet`, `vähintään 5 vuoden kokemus`, Indeed's `Experience: Python: 5 years` footer, and the same shapes in 13 EU languages, including number words and year adjectives (`five years`, `drei Jahre`, `trois ans`, `dreijährige`, `triennale`, `dwuletnie`, `διετής`). Ranges use the minimum (`3–5`, `2/3 anni`, `zwischen 2 und 4 Jahren`). The highest stated requirement is used. Company blurbs are ignored (`we have 15 years of experience`, `Wir blicken auf 12 Jahre Erfahrung zurück`, `Forte de 12 ans d'expérience, notre agence…`, anything above 15 years). Jobs that don't mention years stay. No language model is used.
- **Title** — optional. **Empty: any software-development role** — the job title must name one in any EU language: developer, programmer, software engineer, frontend / backend / fullstack, mobile (iOS, Android, Flutter), DevOps, SRE, data / analytics / ML / AI / cloud / platform engineer, QA engineer and test automation, game, gameplay, embedded and firmware software, software or cloud architect, tech lead, engineering manager, VP or head of engineering, CTO (`Softwareentwickler`, `Fachinformatiker Anwendungsentwicklung`, `Développeur`, `Intégrateur Web`, `Ingénieur Études et Développement`, `Desarrollador`, `Sviluppatore`, `Programista`, `Vývojář`, `Systemutvecklare`, `Mjukvaruingenjör`, `Ohjelmistokehittäjä`, `Szoftvermérnök`, `Μηχανικός Λογισμικού`), and trainees or specialists named after a programming language (`Werkstudent Java`, `PL/SQL Specialist`). Finnish `kehittäjä` and Hungarian `fejlesztő` alone count only next to a software word (`Kehittäjä, Dynamics 365`). Left out: developers of businesses, property, land, energy projects, products, people, policy, courses or events (`Business Developer`, `Projektentwickler`, `forretningsudvikler`, `verksamhetsutvecklare`, `Développeur foncier`, `Desarrollador/a de negocio`, `Personalentwickler`, `Curriculum Developer`, `Music Programmer`), titles where developer is the audience (`Developer Advocate`, `Developer Relations`, `Developer Documentation`), sales / presales / solutions / field / support / applications engineers (`Ingénieur Support Logiciel`, `Field Applications Engineer`; a `Web Application Engineer` counts), IT operations (security and network engineers, and infrastructure or IT systems engineers with no cloud or platform word), manual testers (`Manual & Automation` counts), software jobs that don't build it (`Software Tester`, `Software Asset Manager`), product and project managers, teachers and trainers (`Formateur Développeur Web`, `Docente di Sviluppo Web`), analysts and designers (a `Software Designer` counts). Hardware and electronics, data centres, machine programmers (PLC, CNC) and other engineering disciplines (mechanical, civil, electrical, `Bauingenieur`…) count only when the title also names software work — software, firmware, web, app, iOS / Android, a programming language or test automation (`Firmware-/Elektronikentwickler`, `Senior iOS Engineer – Connected Hardware`); so do one-word compounds whose stem names something other than software (`Verfahrensentwickler`, `Hankekehittäjä`, `Entwicklungsingenieur Mobile Arbeitsmaschinen`; `Systemutvecklare`, `Spieleentwickler` and `Dateningenieur` count). An industry or a business area doesn't make a developer another job (`Android-Entwickler Automotive`, `Data Engineer – Renewable Energy`, `.NET Developer – Commercial Insurance`, `Senior Software Engineer, Developer Experience`). **With a title**, every word must be in the job title, except that next to another word developer, engineer and programmer name the same job when the job is a software role (`Software Engineer` also finds `Software Developer`, `Softwareentwickler` and `Ingénieur logiciel`; `Backend Developer` finds `Backend Engineer`; `Senior Developer` finds `Senior Software Engineer` but not `Senior Sales Engineer`); separate titles with commas to get any of them (`Backend Developer, Platform Engineer`), and each title is searched on its own. Gender forms you type are left out (`Softwareentwickler*in`, `Entwickler:in`, `Développeur·se`, `Programista/ka`, `(m/w/d)`), and a compound also matches the title written in parts (`Softwarearchitekt` = `Software-Architekt`).
- **Stack** — keywords such as `React TypeScript`. Indeed is searched with title + stack, and each stack word must also be a whole word in the title or description (`Go` ≠ `Google`, `Java` ≠ `JavaScript`, `Next.js` ≠ "next", `Node.js` = `NodeJS`, `Python` matches `Python3`). The description is not written to the sheet.
- **OK** — opens a runner tab that fetches in-browser and saves `.xlsx`
- **Check sources** — opens the runner in check mode: every source this version has, ticked or not, is asked for up to 3 jobs from the last 7 days with your countries (Title, Stack and Years are not used), and each says **OK** with how many it scanned and kept, or **FAILED** and why. Nothing is downloaded. Use **Copy report** afterwards to send what each source answered.

Title and stack words are compared without case or accents in any EU language (`Développeur` = `developpeur`, `Straße` = `strasse`, `Łódź` = `lodz`), with `Sr` = `Senior`, `Front-End` = `Frontend`, `engineer` also matching `engineering`, the EU words for developer, engineer and software read as the English ones (`Backend-Entwickler`, `Développeur Backend` and `Programista Backend` all match `Backend Developer`), longer words also inside German compounds (`kubernetes` matches `Kubernetesplattform`), and gender tags like `(m/w/d)`, `(H/F)` or `Entwickler*in` ignored.

## Notes

- **Skip jobs I already downloaded** (popup, on by default): every job you download is remembered in this browser for 31 days (a day longer than the longest Posted window), by its link and by its title + company (so the same job found later in another country or on another board counts too). Later runs leave those jobs out, and each board is asked for extra results so you still get up to Count new jobs per board. If nothing new turns up, the run says "No new jobs" and no file is downloaded. Untick the box to include repeats, or click **Forget downloaded jobs** to start fresh.
- The same job found on several sources, or posted in several countries, is one row: title + company without its legal form (`Acme GmbH` = `Acme`) and gender tags (`(m/w/d)`); for a job whose employer is hidden, its link. The copy kept is the source's with the lowest priority number (company career pages 10, Indeed 20, job boards 30, Hacker News 40, freelance 50, agencies 60), newest first among equals; it takes a direct link from the others if it has none, and **Also on** names the sources it was also found on. The run summary says how many were merged.
- **Results by source**: each source's line as it finishes ("Himalayas: 120 scanned → 14 kept. Left out: …", or "Himalayas failed: HTTP 403 (blocked) at himalayas.app/jobs/api/search"), then "N duplicates merged, M already downloaded". A green dot is done, amber stopped early (the line says why: blocked, rate-limited, its time budget, Stop), red failed, grey skipped. While sources run, a line under the status says which ("Running: Indeed, Himalayas · 1 of 2 done · 0 failed").
- **Stop and download**: ends every source at once; each keeps what it found so far and the file is saved with those jobs. Sources also stop on their own after their time budget (Indeed 5 minutes, boards 2 minutes) and the whole run after 10 minutes.
- **Copy report** (after a run or a check): copies the log, each source's line, and for sources that failed or kept nothing the start of what their site answered (status, a few headers, the first 2 KB; never cookies) — send it when a source fails so it can be fixed from the real answer.
- **Polite fetching**: one request at a time per site, with a pause after each (1 s unless the source says otherwise, or the site's robots.txt `Crawl-delay` when longer, at most 10 s); sources that read pages rather than an API follow robots.txt. A failed request is retried once; a site that answers 429 with a short `Retry-After` is waited for once; 403, 503 or a lasting 429 count as blocked.
- **Run log**: after each country the log says how many jobs Indeed returned and how many were kept, and each source's line says what was left out and why (`not matching the title`, `not developer roles`, `older than 24 hours`, `expired`, `other job types`, `asking for more years`, `without the stack words`, `hybrid or not remote`, `placed in other countries`, `in countries not ticked under Employers abroad`, `in your country`, `only for people living in their country`, `limited to other countries`, `not open to Europe`). When fewer than Count are found it suggests the settings that would widen the next run (a longer Posted window, more Platforms, an empty Title, fewer Stack words, a higher Years, Employers abroad, Type: Both); when a source stopped early (blocked or rate-limited) it says to wait and run again instead.
- **Who can take a job** (every source but Indeed, which applies the same rules itself): a hybrid or on-site job is left out (from the source, or from the title, location or phrases such as "this role is hybrid" or "3 days a week in our Berlin office"; not "Hybrid Cloud Engineer" or "we offer a hybrid model"); so is a job listed in your own country, in a European country you didn't tick, or in the UK, Canada or US not ticked under **Employers abroad**. A board's list of countries that may apply opens the job to those countries (none of yours: `limited to other countries`), narrowed by a time-zone window; with **You live in** set, the posting must not say you must live elsewhere. A job listed in a ticked country is open to it. Anything else (outside Europe, or plain "Remote") must say it hires in Europe or your countries, as for **Employers abroad**.
- Boards may rate-limit or change their APIs; failed platforms are listed in the runner log while others still export. A board that stops partway (blocked, timed out, one country failing) keeps what it found and says "stopped early" in the log. If Indeed stops accepting its newer filters, the log says which step it fell back to.
- **403 fixes:** the extension installs `declarativeNetRequest` header rules so its own Indeed API requests get the mobile Indeed App User-Agent (Indeed answers other user agents with 403), and its own Himalayas API requests a plain client name without Origin or Referer. The rules skip requests made by the sites' own pages, so your normal browsing is untouched. After updating, click **Reload** on the extension card (needed once so the rules install).
- Excel is built with SheetJS (`lib/xlsx.mini.min.js`). Columns: Title, Company, Location, Country, Open to, Site (the source: Indeed, Himalayas…), Posted, Remote, Listing type, Experience years, Job URL, Direct URL, Also on (other sources with the same job). Job URL and Direct URL cells are clickable links.
- Tests: `npm test` from the repository root (Node 20+, no install needed). `npm run e2e` loads the extension in Chromium with Playwright and answers every request from the fixtures (nothing reaches the network): a run with a merge, You live in, a failing source, Stop and Check sources.

## Adding a board

A source is one object in `scrapers/sources.js`, one adapter module, its hosts in the manifest and a fixture; nothing else changes.

1. **Adapter** — `scrapers/boards/<id>.js` (or `careers/`, `freelance/`, `agencies/`) exporting `async run(ctx)`:
   - fetch through `ctx.http` only: `ctx.http.json(url)`, `ctx.http.text(url)`, `ctx.http.post(url, body)` (gaps, the per-host queue, retries, robots.txt and response samples come with it); `expectArray(data, "jobs", "<what>")` (`http.js`) turns a changed API into "unexpected response (keys: …)". RSS/Atom: `parseFeed()` (`formats/feed.js`); Next.js pages: `nextData()` / `findDeep()` (`formats/embedded.js`);
   - map each row with `normalizeJob()` (`util.js`) plus `_id` (stable within the source), `_where` — `{ workplace, listedIn, open, emptyOpen, utc, remoteOnly }`, what the source knows about where the job is and who may apply (see `open-to.js`) — and, when known, `_expires` and `anonymous: true` (hidden employer);
   - call `ctx.accept(jobs)` on each page and stop when `ctx.enough()`; it runs every check (date, title, place, stack, type, years) and counts what each left out. List pages without descriptions go through `ctx.screen(rows)` first, and only the survivors' details are fetched. `ctx.fail(err)` records a part that failed (one query) while the rest goes on; `ctx.note(msg)` and `ctx.progress(msg)` write to the log; `ctx.cache` keeps answers a site may be asked for only so often; `ctx.part(name)` gives each company of a careers source its own Count. Throw when the source can't go on: what was accepted is kept (incomplete), or the source failed.
2. **Registry** — one object in `SOURCES` (`scrapers/sources.js`): `id`, `label`, `group`, `hint`, `url`, `module`, `hosts`, `defaultOn`, and when they differ from the group's: `priority`, `budgetMs`, `gaps` (ms per host), `robots: "obey"` (pages rather than a documented API), `countries` and `countryBound` (sources that only search some countries), `headerRule`.
3. **Manifest** — each host in `host_permissions`; a header rule in `rules/headers.json` only if the site refuses the extension's own requests (ids 3–9 are kept for Referer/Origin rules), with `excludedInitiatorDomains` so the site's own pages are untouched.
4. **Tests** — `tests/fixtures/sources/<id>/routes.json` and body files written from the source's documented answers, with dates as `{{ago:2h|unix}}` tokens (`tests/helpers/fixture.js`): 6–10 rows covering a developer job open to Europe, a non-developer, an old one, a US-only one, a hybrid one, one listed in or limited to one country, one that says you must live in Germany, and a duplicate of another source's job. `tests/src-<id>.test.js` runs `adapterContract({ id, empty, secondPage, expect })` (`tests/helpers/contract.js`) plus the source's own mapping tests. `tests/sources-registry.test.js` checks the entry, the manifest and the fixture; `npm run e2e` serves every fixture to the real extension.
5. Sites that only answer inside a real tab (Cloudflare, DataDome) used a background service worker in the US version: see `background.js` (`scrapeInSiteTab`, `runPageScrape`) in the repository's first commit. `title-match.js`, `developer.js`, `eligibility.js`, `residence.js`, `job-filters.js` and `open-to.js` are plain scripts so they can be injected into such tabs.
